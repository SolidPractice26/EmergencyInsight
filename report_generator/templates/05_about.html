How This Report Works

Every number in this report comes from public government records of events that have already happened. Nothing here is a forecast. It shows, in plain language, what this location has been through so you can prepare for it.

How each hazard is rated
1Count the events
Damaging events of each hazard in the county, per square mile: 10 years of records for common hazards, 30 for rare ones.
→
2Compare with the U.S.
Rank the county against every U.S. county for that hazard only: hurricanes against hurricanes, floods against floods.
→
3Rate it
The rank becomes a word and a 0–10 number. History and severity are shown beside it, never mixed in.

Limits to keep in mind
  • Records depend on reporting. Rural and older events are under-reported.
  • Most weather records are by county, so they can't tell one street from another.
  • Flood claims count only insured homes; actual flooding may be higher.
  • The past is a guide, not a guarantee. Climate and new construction change risk.
